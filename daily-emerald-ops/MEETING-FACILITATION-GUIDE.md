# Kickoff Meeting Facilitation Guide

**The Daily Emerald, Operating Structure Proposal**

This guide is your play by play for the kickoff meeting. It is written from a management perspective, and it has one job above all others: to help you present this structure as a **proposal that is open to change, not a finished decree**.

You are leading volunteers, not employees. They are giving you their time for free because they believe in the brand. The fastest way to lose them is to hand down rules. The fastest way to keep them is to make them co authors of the plan. Everything below serves that goal.

> **The single framing rule for the whole meeting:** at every stage, tell the team that their suggestions will be taken into account and folded into the plan, that the Discussion Draft is a living document that will change based on their input, and that nothing is locked until they have weighed in.

All times are in UTC. Ask everyone to convert to their local time before the meeting.

---

## 1. Pre meeting prep

Do these before anyone joins.

- **Pick a time that works for a global, asynchronous team.** Post it in UTC with a note to convert to local. If you cannot get everyone in one call, plan to record it and share the slides and the Discussion Draft straight after.
- **Have your three materials ready:** the slide deck, this guide, and the Discussion Draft (`PROPOSAL-Daily-Emerald-Operations-DRAFT.docx`).
- **Decide how you will capture feedback live.** Simplest option: keep the Discussion Draft open and type notes straight into the feedback and changes log table at the end of it. Alternative: ask one trusted person to be the note taker so you can focus on facilitating.
- **Re read the open question** about a possible third Division Head so you can present it cleanly as a team decision, not your decision.
- **Set the room up:** everyone muted at the start, you ready to share the slides, and the Discussion Draft ready to show after.
- **Get your own head right.** You are not selling a finished system. You are opening a conversation. Aim to talk less than half the meeting.

---

## 2. Timed agenda

A 60 minute shape. Stretch or compress to fit. The key ratio is roughly one third presenting, two thirds discussion.

| Time | Segment | You are |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 min | Welcome and framing | Setting the tone: this is a proposal |
| 3 to 18 min | Muted presentation of the slides | Presenting the proposed structure |
| 18 to 20 min | Unmute and reset the floor | Handing the room to the team |
| 20 to 40 min | Open discussion on the draft | Listening and capturing feedback |
| 40 to 52 min | Open questions slot (third Division Head, team splits) | Putting decisions to the team |
| 52 to 60 min | Close, ownership, and next steps | Confirming what happens next |

---

## 3. What to say during the muted presentation

Keep everyone muted only while you present the slides, so the walkthrough is clean. Lead with the framing before any content.

**Opening lines (say something close to this):**

> "Thanks for being here. Before I start, the most important thing: what I am about to show you is a first draft, not a final decision. I have put together a proposed way for us to organise, but it is a proposal. Nothing is locked. I want your feedback, and your suggestions will be folded into the plan before anything becomes official. So please hold your thoughts while I run through the slides, and then the floor is yours."

Then present the slides in order: why we are doing this, the proposed structure and chain of command, the weekly rhythm, how work flows, the meeting format, the tools, the worked example, and what getting organised unlocks.

**As you go, drop in reminders that this is open:**

- "This is what I am proposing, but tell me later if it should be different."
- "This part especially I want your view on."

**Closing line of the presentation:**

> "That is the proposal. None of it is set. Now I want to hear from you."

---

## 4. Unmute promptly

**Unmute the room as soon as you finish presenting.** Do not keep people muted while you keep talking. On a volunteer team, a long one way broadcast makes a proposal feel like a decree, which is exactly what you are trying to avoid. The moment the slides are done, open the mics and hand over the room.

---

## 5. How to genuinely open the floor

Silence after "any questions?" is normal and does not mean agreement. Ask specific, inviting questions instead. Use these:

- "What in here would you change if it were up to you?"
- "Which team do you see yourself on, and does its charter look right?"
- "Where does this structure feel like it would get in your way?"
- "What did I miss, or get wrong?"
- "Is there a team or a role we are missing?"

Make it safe to disagree. When someone offers a change, thank them out loud and say you are writing it into the draft right now. People propose more when they see the first suggestion actually land.

---

## 6. How to capture feedback live

- Keep the Discussion Draft open at the **feedback and changes log** table.
- For each point, record **who raised it**, the **requested change**, and the **decision** (agreed, parked, or needs more discussion).
- Say it as you type: "Good, I am putting that in the log now." This shows the document really is living.
- You do not have to resolve everything in the room. "Parked, we will work it out" is a fine outcome. Capture it and move on.

---

## 7. Handling pushback, silence, and people who want to lead

**If someone pushes back or objects:**

- Treat it as a gift, not a threat. It means they care.
- Reflect it back: "So your concern is X, have I got that right?"
- Fold it in if it is sound: "That is better, let us change it."
- If you disagree, explain the why, then offer to park it: "I see it differently, but let us note it and come back to it rather than decide now."
- Never win the argument at the cost of the person. You need them more than you need to be right.

**If the room goes silent:**

- Do not fill it immediately. Count to five.
- Narrow the question: instead of "any thoughts?", ask "[Name], you would be on the Content Team, does that charter look right to you?"
- Offer a low stakes entry: "Even a small thing, what is one word you would change?"
- If it stays quiet, invite written feedback after: "No pressure now, drop comments in the draft or message me later. It all goes in."

**If someone wants to be a lead:**

- Welcome it warmly. Enthusiasm to lead is exactly what you need.
- Remind everyone of the two leadership requirements: 18 or older, and a working microphone, since leadership coordination happens in voice meetings.
- Do not appoint on the spot unless you are sure. Say: "Great, note your interest, I will confirm leads after we have settled the structure." This keeps it fair and keeps your options open.
- If several people want the same role, that is a good problem. Park it and decide after the meeting.

---

## 8. The open questions slot

Give this its own agenda slot so it does not get lost. The main item is the **possible third Division Head**.

Put it to the team, do not decide it for them:

> "Here is something I genuinely want us to decide together. As proposed, two Division Heads each oversee four teams. That might be too much for two people. One option is to add a third Division Head and regroup the eight teams across three divisions instead of two. I have not decided this. What do you think?"

Then walk through it:

- "Do three divisions make more sense than two?"
- "If we did three, how would you split the eight teams across them?"
- "Who here might want to step up as a Head?"

Capture the outcome in the feedback log. It is fine to leave the exact split to be worked out together after the meeting. The point is that the team owns this call.

---

## 9. How to close

- **Summarise what you heard.** Read back the headline changes from the feedback log so people see their input landed.
- **Confirm ownership and next steps.** Name the teams, and note where leads are confirmed or still open (use [Name TBD] where undecided).
- **Make the next step concrete and reassuring:**

> "Here is what happens next. I am going to update this draft to reflect everything we agreed today. The final handbook and docs will be built from that approved version, so what we decided here is what ships. I will share the updated draft so you can check I captured it right."

- **Thank them.** They gave you their time. Say so plainly.

---

## 10. Guidance specific to leading volunteers

Keep these in mind throughout:

- **Recognition is your main currency.** You cannot pay people, so credit, titles, and visible appreciation matter more, not less. Use them generously.
- **Respect their time.** Keep the meeting tight, start and end on schedule, and never waste their hours.
- **Clarity beats control.** Volunteers will follow a clear, fair system far more readily than a set of orders.
- **Momentum keeps people.** Small early wins matter. The worked example and a quick first task help people feel the structure working.
- **Consent, not compliance.** You want people nodding because they helped build this, not because they were told to. That is the whole reason this is a proposal.
- **Frame the rhythm professionally.** Present the weekly cadence as a sensible operating model and the back half of the week as you focusing on brand and production while the leadership team executes. Keep it about the work.
